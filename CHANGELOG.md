## Unreleased

- Completed `to-do-ui` Spec 6 Retire the Experiment and Close Out ([feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260924%5DP6_to-do-ui-retire-experiment-and-close-out.md)), which ends the initiative. The `apple-reminders-ux` prototype is **deleted** — 47 files under `components/web_client/src/experiment/apple-reminders-ux/`, its registry entry, and the route `/exp/apple-reminders-ux`, which now reports no experiment at that path rather than erroring. All three of its iterations shipped into production across Specs 1-5, so what remained was a second, diverging implementation of the app the user actually runs. Its artifacts moved to `docs/experiments/archive/apple-reminders-ux/`, screenshots included, each with a recorded verdict naming the Spec that absorbed it. **No production module changed** — nothing under `src/components/`, `src/hooks/`, `src/services/`, `src/sync/`, or `src/preferences/` — which is the deletion's own proof that the `/exp` seam held: the experiment was parallel to production for its whole life, never a fork of it. The `/exp` infrastructure stays registered-but-empty for the next experiment, and its tests are re-fixtured onto a neutral `sample-experiment` slug so they no longer name a deleted tree; the two experiment-tools end-to-end checks that need a _named_ prototype are committed skipped, while the two that only need `/exp` — which arms the tools just as an experiment does — stay active alongside a new check on the empty registry itself. `docs/DESIGN.md` needed two corrections rather than a rewrite — the pixel source-of-truth path follows the archive, and the Accessibility section stopped calling the contrast exception unresolved, a decision made on 2026-09-21 and shipped in Spec 5. The initiative retrospective at [`docs/retrospectives/2026-09-24-to-do-ui.md`](docs/retrospectives/2026-09-24-to-do-ui.md) carries six refactor proposals for seams the experiment's "no production-module edits" rule kept closed for its whole life, none of them implemented here.

- Acting on one task no longer means entering a mode built for batches ([#127](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/127); [PR #140](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/140); [feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260923%5DGH127_to-do-ui-task-item-details-drawer.md)). Tapping a task title still focuses it for editing, and now also reveals an **(i)** button in the trailing column the drag handle occupies in selection mode — the two are mutually exclusive by construction, so a tap there means one thing at a time. It opens a **Task Item Drawer** holding an editable title, the task's `Completed`, `Last updated` and `Created` timestamps, and a delete; a task that is not finished shows an em dash for Completed rather than a blank row, which would read as a surface that failed to load. Its header is `MoveToListDrawer`'s grammar unchanged — ✕ discards and closes, ✓ commits and closes, Escape reads as ✕ — and ✓ stays inert until the title actually differs. **Deleting one task takes two presses**, the same grammar the selection bar's batch delete uses: the first arms the same control with a destructive ring and the label `Delete Task?`, the second performs it, and editing the title retires the arming because it changes what the press would destroy. The deletion is the ordinary soft delete, so the task lands in Recently Deleted like any other. **Tapping a title also stopped moving the row.** The inline edit field was an `<input>`, which cannot wrap: a two-line title collapsed to one and the row lost 22px under the user's finger, pulling everything below it up — the stylesheet had matched font, size, line-height and padding between the rendered title and the field all along, so `overflow-wrap` and `white-space` were simply dead rules on an element incapable of honouring them. The field is now an auto-growing `<textarea>` wearing the title's own class, sized from its measured `scrollHeight`, so the row keeps its height at the moment of the tap and grows only as typed text needs another line; Enter still ends the edit rather than inserting a newline. `useInlineEdit` widened over both element types instead of forcing its other call site to follow — the list-title editor keeps an `<input>`, since a list name is one line by design. Two adjacent fixes ride along: **both drawers are now modal in fact and not only in ARIA**, sitting in the scrim `DeleteListDialog` already used, so the list and the new-task button are covered and unreachable behind an open drawer instead of floating over it; and **both become centred modals past 768px**, the shell's existing breakpoint, because a bottom-anchored sheet sits far from where a wide-window user is looking. Move to List inherits both for free — the placement they share moved into `styles/drawer.css`, and neither drawer's own stylesheet now says anything about where it sits. No backend change was needed or made: the three timestamps already ship on the `Task` payload, and the drawer's delete reuses the soft-delete path a batch delete already calls one task at a time, inheriting the offline queue and its recovery.

- Fixed three things the batch-edit and list-delete surfaces got wrong ([#138](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/138); [PR #139](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/139); [feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260923%5DGH138_to-do-ui-bulk-edit-and-list-deletion-fixes.md)), all found by the operator after [#126](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/126) shipped. **Batch completion is now a toggle.** It only ever marked complete: a pooled task that was already done was filtered out of the batch, so there was no way to reopen several tasks at once and pressing the action on a finished pool did nothing at all. One press now flips each pooled task to its own other state, which means a mixed pool inverts — the finished rows reopen and the open ones close — rather than resolving to one shared outcome. The glyph is unchanged, because a per-row inversion has no single direction to draw; the accessible name carries it (`Toggle completion of selected tasks`). **The armed delete is a ring, not a fill.** Arming the batch trash flooded the whole button with `--destructive` and inverted the glyph, which read as a different, louder control rather than the same one waiting; it is now a 2px inset ring around the icon, with the glyph staying red. **Deleting a list asks what happens to the items.** It used to take a second press on the menu item, signalled by nothing but a `?` appended to the label, and said nothing about the tasks inside — which were silently re-homed to the default list by the service's orphan sweep (`task-orphan-sweep.ts`), a behaviour no part of the UI ever mentioned. One press now closes the menu and opens a dialog naming the list and its item count, offering **Delete With Items**, **Delete Just the List**, and **Cancel**; an empty list is offered a single deletion, because with nothing inside the two outcomes are identical. Cancel holds the initial focus and sits last, so no keypress on a freshly-opened dialog destroys anything. **Recently Deleted now says what each row was** — a kind glyph plus `task`, or `list · N items` for a list deleted with its tasks — and restoring that list brings those tasks back with it, in it. The cascaded tasks keep their own rows too, so a single task can still be rescued out of a list the user meant to delete; restored alone while its list is still deleted, it lands in the default list. Neither outcome needed a schema change or an endpoint: both are client-orchestrated batches over the calls a single row already uses, so both inherit the offline queue. Which tasks were deleted _with_ a list is derived rather than stored — the cascade issues one `deleted_at` for the list and every task in it — which keeps a task deleted on its own an hour earlier out of the list's count and out of its restore. Two service constraints shaped that: a tombstoned task cannot be re-pointed, because `patchTask` clears `deletedAt` on every patch and would resurrect it, so **Delete Just the List** moves only live tasks; and `deleteTask` clamps its `deleted_at` through `clampFutureTimestamp` while `deleteList` does not, so a client whose clock runs ahead of the server can desync the pair and leave a cascaded task ungrouped — it still appears on its own row and is still restorable, so the failure is cosmetic. One test-harness fix rides along: stories that render the whole app now import `styles/tokens.css`, which only `main.tsx` had been importing, so every such story had been running with no design tokens at all and no assertion on a token-derived colour could ever have held.

- Selecting and editing several tasks at once is now a mode on the list itself ([#126](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/126); [PR #134](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/134); [feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260923%5DGH126_to-do-ui-task-selection-and-batch-editing.md)). **Select Items** in the list options menu hands the header over to the mode — the sync control steps aside, a filled accent checkmark is the one way out — and turns every row into something that pools rather than edits: the mark or the title adds a task to the batch, and a tick in the circle now means "selected" and nothing else, which is why a completed task is drawn as a ring with a filled core rather than the solid disc it used to be. A flat action bar takes the plane the new-task button occupies, arriving with the mode rather than with the first selection and reading dimmed with its actions disabled until something is pooled, and applies **complete**, **move**, or **delete** to the whole batch. Deleting takes two presses, as deleting a list does: the first arms the action — open-lidded trash, filled destructive pill, accessible name `Confirm deleting N tasks` — and a changed pool, another batch action, or leaving the mode disarms it. **Move** opens a from-bottom drawer listing every list except the one in view. Open tasks also carry a drag handle: pointer-driven so it works on an iPhone, where HTML5 drag never fires, with ArrowUp/ArrowDown on a focused handle as the pointer-free equivalent; completed tasks keep their completion order and carry no handle. While the mode is on, the list name itself is editable in the header — the first consumer of `useLists().renameList`, which had shipped unused. **Hand order is deliberately not persisted yet**: `Task` has no `position`, and the operator chose to judge the interaction before the contract was designed, so a drag survives re-renders and re-sorts but not a reload, which falls back to today's `created_at` order. Persisting it is outstanding work that mirrors List ordering exactly — `tasks.position` as a `COLLATE "C"` fractional index with its shared-type, migration, IndexedDB, and sync work. Complete, move, and delete needed no backend change at all: `TaskPatchInputSchema` already carried `completed_at` and `list_id`, and each batch action goes one task at a time through the same client paths a single row uses, so every one inherits the offline queue and its recovery. A batch is issued at once and awaited as a whole, so the pool is frozen while it is in flight and one failed write never strands the tasks behind it; when everything settles the mode hands the user back to the ordinary list with the affected rows already showing their new state. Bulk edits still have no contract of their own — a batch is N independent writes, atomic nowhere, and a partial failure is silent about which write did not land ([#137](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/137)). The task row was split along the way into a presentation shell plus `EditableTaskRow` and `SelectableTaskRow`, with the drag handle hanging off the shell's reorder props rather than off selection mode, so ordinary rows can become draggable later without a second copy of it, and editing a stored string in place — a task's title, a list's name — is now one hook (`useInlineEdit`) instead of a copy per call site.
- PSYKL has its own mark, everywhere it appears ([#125](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/125); [PR #133](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/133); [feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DGH125_to-do-ui-psykl-logo-and-app-identity.md)). The browser tab previously showed nothing at all: `index.html` pointed `rel="icon"` at `/pwa-icon.png`, a file that had never existed in `public/`. The tab now carries `favicon.svg`, which holds its own `prefers-color-scheme` rule and so inverts to white ink on a dark tab strip, with `favicon.ico` as the fallback. In the shell, the mobile header and the open sidebar lead with the mark — inlined SVG filled with `currentColor`, so one asset serves every theme and both contrast modes — and past 768 px, where the sidebar is permanent and cannot be closed, its close control is replaced by a static brand heading that is out of the tab order rather than merely invisible. Every rendition was reframed: the delivered pack drew the mark at ~54% of its canvas, which reads as a speck once iOS adds tile padding and a browser shrinks the favicon to 16 px, so the iOS tile is now 82%, the Android `any` icons 86%, and the favicon 94%. Maskable icons stay at 56% on purpose — a launcher guarantees only the centre 80% circle, so a square mark cannot exceed 80%/√2 there — and the manifest no longer claims `any maskable` on artwork with no safe zone, which had been letting Android crop into the mark. **The installed home-screen tile follows Settings → Appearance:** Safari resolves no dark variant of `apple-touch-icon` for a web app, but it does read that link out of the live document at _Add to Home Screen_ time, so the app points it at the matching tile — explicit Light or Dark wins, System defers to the device. An icon already on the home screen never changes; iOS bakes it at add time. That exposed a pre-existing defect fixed here: `data-theme` was only stamped when `SettingsView` mounted, so a saved Dark appearance was ignored on every load until the user reopened Settings — preferences now apply from `main.tsx`. Renditions are generated by a committed script (`pnpm --filter @psykl/web-client icons:generate`) from `assets/logo/source/psykl-icon.svg`, now committed as the source of truth.

- Comparing a prototype against the shipped app is now a two-tap switch from wherever the 🧪 button is visible ([#123](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/123); [PR #132](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/132); [feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DGH123_to-do-ui-experiment-tools-toggle.md)). The expanded pill names the experience on screen — **Production**, **Experiments**, or the experiment's own title, where it used to read a static "Not production" — and that name opens a picker offering Production first and always, then every registered experiment. Opening any `/exp` surface turns the tools on for that device, so they ride along over production routes until **Close** puts them away; a developer who has never opened an experiment sees no chrome at all, which is why the pill is neither always-on nor gated to dev builds — the homelab deploy is where phone testing happens. Previously the only way back into a prototype was closing the experiment, navigating to the production view, and going back through Settings → Experiments. Two adjacent fixes land with it: the experiments list takes its colours from the theme tokens, where the title had inherited the user-agent button colour and rendered black-on-black under Dark appearance, and each experiment is now one bordered full-width row rather than a run of text. The registry's `status` property is deleted — every exit in the lightweight workflow ends with the experiment's code and entry removed, so a registered experiment is by definition being explored and the field could only ever read `exploring`. `Root.tsx` is the only production module touched: one conditional mount, with the `no-restricted-imports` boundary to the experiment tree unchanged.

- An installed PWA can now update itself from Settings → About ([#124](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/124); [PR #131](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/131); [feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DGH124_to-do-ui-in-app-pwa-update.md)). The block reads `Current Version:` in plain language instead of two raw commits, and one button carries the interaction — **Check for updates** re-checks in place, becomes **Update to Latest** when a newer bundle is found, and otherwise stamps `Last checked:` under itself. Pressing it activates the waiting service worker and reloads onto the new bundle, landing back on `/settings`. The available version is shown only while it differs from the loaded one. Until now a new deploy was unreachable from inside an iOS home-screen PWA: `src/sw.ts` claims clients but never calls `skipWaiting()`, so the replacement worker waited for every client to close, which on an installed PWA effectively never happens, and the only escape was deleting the bookmark or clearing website data. The available version comes from a build-stamped `/version.json` emitted outside the service worker's precache and served `no-store`; `sw.js` is now served `no-cache` as well, having previously been caught by the one-year immutable asset rule that would have prevented the browser from ever noticing a new worker. The service-task API is unchanged and its build commit stays as a provenance detail below. `components/VersionFooter/` is renamed to `components/AppVersion/`. The same PR steadies the CI component job: `PendingQueuedTask`'s wait budget goes from 3s to 8s (it waits on an affordance deliberately delayed 2s, leaving about a second of slack on a contended runner), and the job retries the whole command up to three times — per-test `jest.retryTimes` was measured reporting a story rigged to always throw as **passed** on its retry, so it was rejected rather than shipped.

- Brought the production web client into parity with the accepted Apple Reminders UX prototype ([#122](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/122); [feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DGH122_to-do-ui-production-prototype-parity.md)): task rows and capture separators use the prototype's spacing, capture floats above the safe area, the Lists action is a circled icon, Settings uses segmented controls and contains the version readout under About, and task rows no longer show Delete. Sync copy remains `Everything is synced.`

- Renamed the Experimental lane to the **Lightweight feature workflow** and gave it two targets, so the same small planning kit — feature card, one visual artifact, acceptance checks, implementation notes — now serves both prototyping and fast iteration on shipped code. `target = prototype` is the old lane unchanged: artifacts in `docs/experiments/{slug}/{iteration}/`, code confined to a client's `src/experiment/` tree, reachable at `/exp/{slug}`, reduced test floor. `target = production` is new: artifacts in `docs/specs/{initiative}/{YYYYMMDD}-{slug}/` (a _folder_, where a heavyweight Spec is a _file_, so the two coexist without competing for Spec numbering), code in the real production modules, one branch and one PR with no Spec integration branch and no DevTask stack. The reduction is planning ceremony only — **the production target keeps the full five-layer test pyramid, TDD ordering, and the same-PR E2E mandate**, because a user reaches that code the moment it merges. Escalation to [`production-dev-workflow.md`](docs/workflows/production-dev-workflow.md) is required when work introduces a domain concept, spans components by contract, needs design review, or is a prototype promotion of more than a thin surface; a backend change discovered mid-flight must be raised with the operator rather than absorbed. `docs/workflows/experimental-feature-workflow.md` is renamed to [`docs/workflows/lightweight-feature-workflow.md`](docs/workflows/lightweight-feature-workflow.md).

## [0.5.0] - 2026-09-22

- Fixed three defects found while stabilising the end-to-end suite ([#116](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/116), [#118](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/118); PRs [#113](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/113), [#119](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/119)). **Cross-origin writes never left the device:** the service advertised only `Content-Type` and `X-User-Id` in its CORS `Access-Control-Allow-Headers` while every mutation carries an `Idempotency-Key`, so the browser refused each write at the preflight — silently in both directions, since the optimistic write made the UI look right and a blocked preflight never reaches a server log. Latent rather than active on `robin`, whose client calls the API same-origin at `/api` and therefore issues no preflight, but live in local development, LAN device testing, and any future split of the ingress. The allowed-header list now lives in one testable place with a preflight contract test over it. **Re-ordering lists silently did nothing** once two lists shared an ordering position: a new list took its position from the hook's render snapshot, which lags what IndexedDB already holds, and two equal keys cannot be split — the throw was then discarded by a `void`, so the arrows were dead with no message and no recovery. Positions are now derived from storage, a re-order that cannot be expressed re-spaces the lists and heals a device already holding a duplicate, and the default "Tasks" list can no longer go missing when a user adds a list before the bootstrap runs. **A queued operation could be stranded forever:** replay is woken only by a user action, an `online` event, or the page becoming visible, so an operation enqueued while a replay was already running fired a wake-up the replay lock swallowed, and the pass in flight had already read the queue. Hydration also no longer absorbs server rows over records this device has changed but not yet sent, and a task captured before the lists finish loading lands in the default list rather than in no list at all. [#117](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/issues/117) — the default list id colliding across accounts — remains open and is scheduled with `multi-tenant-auth`.

- End-to-end tests now start against a service that holds nothing. Playwright already gives each test a fresh browser context; the service was the one thing every test shared, so a list created by one test hydrated into the next one's assertions. A test-only reset route, opened by an environment variable set solely in the end-to-end compose overlay and answering `404` everywhere a user can reach, wipes server state between tests — per-user isolation being blocked for Lists by #117. Storybook's own isolation was silently broken too: `deleteDB` is blocked while any connection is open, so stories had been inheriting each other's rows.

- Completed `to-do-ui` Spec 5 Sync, Recently Deleted, and Settings ([feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DP5_to-do-ui-sync-recently-deleted-and-settings.md)): Sync is now a destination rather than a banner, listing what has not reached the server, what was refused and why, and which of this device's edits another device replaced. A conflict record keeps **both** versions — the change this device wrote and the one that superseded it — collapsed until opened, dismissible, and otherwise kept for 30 days, the same window Recently Deleted uses. Both halves were already in hand at replay time, so nothing about the wire format or the schema changed. Settings gained device-local Appearance (System / Light / Dark) and Contrast (Standard / Increased), composing into four combinations against the token sheet Spec 1 shipped. `Toast` and `OutOfSyncBanner` are deleted: a conflict is a fact about data, not an event, and a banner that vanishes is no use hours later — the offline nag and the write ceiling keep their behaviour and move their signal to the header sync control. Constituent PRs: [#111](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/111), [#112](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/112), [#113](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/115).

- Completed `to-do-ui` Spec 4 List Options and Completed Visibility ([feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DP4_to-do-ui-list-options-and-completed-visibility.md)): a per-list overflow menu in the header now holds show/hide completed and delete list, restoring the list-delete affordance that Spec 1 removed with `ListSwitcher`. Whether a list shows its completed tasks is remembered per list in `sync_meta` — device-local, never enqueued, with a unit test holding that line — and the prototype's `localStorage` store was rewritten onto it rather than ported. Deleting a list takes a second tap rather than a dialog, and the only list a user has cannot be deleted. Constituent PRs: [#109](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/109), [#110](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/110).

- Completed `to-do-ui` Spec 3 Inline Capture ([feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DP3_to-do-ui-inline-capture.md)): capture moved from a form at the top of the page into the list itself. An add control opens an empty row after the last open task — so a new task never lands beneath the completed ones — where Return saves and immediately offers the next row, Escape leaves, and an empty row is discarded when it loses focus. The input takes focus in `useLayoutEffect` rather than via `autoFocus`, which focuses after paint and dropped the first characters of anything typed straight away. That regression is guarded in a real browser, because jsdom focuses `autoFocus` synchronously and cannot tell the two implementations apart — verified by reverting the component and watching the jsdom test still pass. `components/TaskCreateForm/` is deleted, and the offline write ceiling that lived in its input now refuses capture at the add control. Constituent PRs: [#107](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/107), [#108](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/108).

- Completed `to-do-ui` Spec 2 Task List and Row ([feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260922%5DP2_to-do-ui-task-list-and-row.md)): the task row is now the prototype's row language — a 22px circle that fills with the tint and strikes the title (honouring `prefers-reduced-motion`), 17px titles that wrap rather than truncate with the checkbox and pending dot pinned to a wrapped title's first line, hairline separators, and no inline styles left in the row. Ordering changed: open tasks read oldest-first so a newly captured task lands next to where the user is typing, and completed tasks sink below them, most recently completed first — derived at render by `sortTasks`, never persisted. A failed refresh is now silent while the device still has tasks to show. The row keeps its delete affordance, which the prototype never had, because no later Spec reintroduces one and removing it would drop a shipped user story. Constituent PRs: [#105](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/105), [#106](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/106).

- Completed `to-do-ui` Spec 1 Shell, Navigation, and List Management ([feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260921%5DP1_to-do-ui-shell-navigation-and-lists.md)): the accepted `apple-reminders-ux` prototype's chrome is now production. A responsive `AppShell` gives a dismissible drawer at phone width and a permanent sidebar at desktop width; every destination — the active list, Lists, Sync, Recently Deleted, Settings — is URL-backed, so each is linkable and the browser's back button is the navigation history. The header carries a sync control reading clear or needs attention from the queued and permanently-failed counts together. List management moved out of a modal onto a Lists page (create, re-order, open), retiring `components/ListSwitcher/` entirely — which means list **rename** and **delete** are unavailable on `/` from this Spec onward, both accepted and tracked (delete returns in Spec 4, rename after Spec 6). A production token sheet carries Standard and Increased contrast in light and dark; the Settings control selecting between them lands in Spec 5. The new-list input focuses in `useLayoutEffect` rather than via `autoFocus`, a fix earned in the prototype's review where `autoFocus` landed after paint and silently dropped the first characters typed. Constituent PRs: [#100](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/100), [#101](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/101), [#103](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/103), [#104](https://github.com/jonpham/PSYKL-System/pull/104).

- Split the AI working agreement into two lanes and added an experiment sandbox to `web_client`. `AGENTS.md` drops from 442 to ~292 lines: the production Spec ceremony (planning, execution gating, File & Status Discipline, subagent dispatch, design-doc rules, skill transitions) moved verbatim to [`docs/workflows/production-dev-workflow.md`](docs/workflows/production-dev-workflow.md), and a `## Workflow Routing` section now names both lanes. The Experimental lane ([`docs/workflows/lightweight-feature-workflow.md`](docs/workflows/lightweight-feature-workflow.md)) trades that ceremony for four short artifacts — feature card, one visual artifact, 3-8 acceptance checks, implementation notes — templated under `docs/templates/` and landed in `docs/experiments/{slug}/`. `web_client` gained `src/experiment/` reachable at `/exp/{slug}` via a hand-rolled path dispatcher (`usePathname.ts` + `Root.tsx`, no routing dependency), an `ExperimentFrame` "not production" banner, a `registry.ts` where adding an experiment is one line, and a new Settings panel as the only way in. ## [0.4.0] - 2026-09-15

- Completed `todo-experience` Spec 2 Recently Deleted + Offline Posture ([feature doc](docs/features/%5B20260818%5DP2_todo-experience-recently-deleted-and-offline-posture.md)): a delete on the client is now a _move_, not a destroy — deleted Tasks and Lists surface in a "Recently Deleted" screen for 30 days with a days-remaining count and can be restored; the server hard-deletes tombstoned rows only after 30 days via a scheduled purge job; dangling `Task.list_id` references self-heal on read via an orphan sweep rather than a foreign key. Offline stops being unbounded: a banner nags at 25 queued-but-unsynced changes, and new writes are refused past a hard ceiling of 100, with the capture field disabled and reading "Reconnect to keep adding."
